Tensors and Their Transformations

18 February 2024, revised 25 October 2026. Level: undergraduate.

This post introduces the definition of a Cartesian tensor and the intuition behind it, then works through a change of basis in an example.

Definition

Consider a rotation of the basis \(\{\mathbf{e}_i\}\) (frame \(S\)) to a new basis \(\{\mathbf{e}'_i\}\) (frame \(S'\)). This is a passive rotation: the vectors stay put and the axes move. Define the transformation matrix

\[\ell_{ij} = \mathbf{e}'_i \cdot \mathbf{e}_j .\]

The components of any vectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) in the two frames are related by

\[a'_i = \ell_{ij}\, a_j, \qquad b'_i = \ell_{ij}\, b_j,\]

with summation over repeated indices. A tensor of rank \(n\) is a set of components that transform in the same way in every index:

\[T'_{ij\cdots k} = \ell_{ip}\, \ell_{jq} \cdots \ell_{kr}\, T_{pq\cdots r}.\]

For a rank-2 tensor this is

\[T'_{ij} = \ell_{ip}\, \ell_{jq}\, T_{pq} = \ell_{ip}\, T_{pq}\, (L^{T})_{qj},\]

which in matrix form reads

\[T'=L\,T\,L^{T},\]

where \(L\) is the matrix with entries \(\ell_{ij}\).

Another way to see the transformation law

A rank-2 tensor \(T\) can be thought of as a linear map that sends a vector \(\mathbf{v}\) to \(T\mathbf{v}\). In the new frame, the same map sends \(\mathbf{v}'\) to \(T'\mathbf{v}'\).

To compute \(T\mathbf{v}\) we may instead:

  1. rotate into the new basis with \(L\);
  2. apply \(T'\);
  3. rotate back with \(L^{-1}\).

This gives \(T\mathbf{v}=L^{-1}T'L\,\mathbf{v}\) for every \(\mathbf{v}\), so \(T'=L\,T\,L^{-1}\). Rotation matrices are orthogonal, \(L^{-1}=L^{T}\), so this agrees with the index calculation above.

Example: an inertia tensor

The principal moments of inertia of a rigid body about the axes \(\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \mathbf{e}_3\) are \(1\), \(2\) and \(3\). An observer uses the basis

\[\begin{aligned} \mathbf{e}_1'&=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathbf{e}_1+ \mathbf{e}_2\right), \\ \mathbf{e}_2'&=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-\mathbf{e}_1+ \mathbf{e}_2\right), \\ \mathbf{e}_3'&=\mathbf{e}_3 . \end{aligned}\]

What components of the inertia tensor does the observer measure?

Solution

From \(\ell_{ij}=\mathbf{e}_i' \cdot \mathbf{e}_j\),

\[L=\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad I=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .\]

It is tempting to transform the inertia tensor like a vector, but it is a rank-2 tensor, so the transformation law applies to each index:

\[I_{i j}^{\prime} =\ell_{i p}\, \ell_{j q}\, I_{p q} =\left(L\, I\, L^T\right)_{i j}.\]

Multiplying out,

\[\begin{aligned} I' &= \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \\ &\qquad\times\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix} \\ &=\begin{pmatrix} \frac{3}{2} & \frac{1}{2} & 0 \\ \frac{1}{2} & \frac{3}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix} . \end{aligned}\]

As a check, \(I'\) is still symmetric, as an inertia tensor must be in any frame, and its trace (the sum of the principal moments, \(6\)) is unchanged.

Exercises

  • What is the rank of a vector? Of a scalar? (Undergraduate, year 2)
  • Show that \(\det L = +1\) for rotations and \(\det L = -1\) for reflections and inversions of the basis. (Undergraduate, years 1–2)
  • Let \(\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\). Show that \(c'_i = (\det L)\, \ell_{ij}\, c_j\) by considering rotations, reflections and inversions. Objects that transform like this are called pseudovectors. (Undergraduate, year 2)
  • Give an example of a pseudovector in physics. (Undergraduate, year 2)
  • () Define the Levi-Civita symbol and show that it is a rank-3 pseudotensor. (Undergraduate, years 2–3*)
  • (*) Give an example of a pseudoscalar in physics. (Hint: helicity.) (Undergraduate, years 3–4*)
