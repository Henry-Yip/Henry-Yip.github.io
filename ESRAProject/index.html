    Pendulum Models for Liquid Slosh

    March 19, 2024 3 minute read Undergraduate

    Liquid motion is hard to analyse in general: it is governed by the Navier–Stokes equations, which have closed-form solutions only in special cases. Building on a published paper, our group modelled the sloshing of a confined liquid at low speeds using two simplified models.

    The models

    The starting point was the paper Modelling Transient Liquid Slosh Behavior at Variable Operating Speeds Induced by Intermittent Motions in Packaging Machines, which represents the liquid surface as a plane that tilts with a single pendulum. In practice the surface does not stay flat, so we proposed two refinements:

    • a discrete model: an \(n\)-pendulum system in which each pendulum tracks two points on the surface, with the surface shape recovered by interpolating between them (the double pendulum is the simplest case); we derived its equations of motion from the Lagrangian;
    • a continuous model: the surface treated like a string vibrating under an oscillating force, which leads to an inhomogeneous wave equation.

    Poster

    We presented the project at a conference. The poster is available here (PDF). It contains some errors, listed in the note at the end; the poster itself is kept as it was. The next section redoes its simulations with corrected equations.

    Redoing the simulations (2026)

    Discrete model

    I derived the equations of motion from the poster’s Lagrangian with its three typing errors corrected:

    \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L} ={}& \left(m+\tfrac12 M\right)\dot x^2 + m\ell\,\dot x\left(2\cos\theta_1\,\dot\theta_1+\cos\theta_2\,\dot\theta_2\right)\\ &+ \tfrac12 m\ell^2\left(2\dot\theta_1^2+\dot\theta_2^2\right) + m\ell^2\,\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\\ &+ mg\ell\left(2\cos\theta_1+\cos\theta_2\right)\\ &+ \tfrac12 d\ell^2\left[\cos^2\theta_1\,\dot\theta_1^2+\left(\cos\theta_1\,\dot\theta_1+\cos\theta_2\,\dot\theta_2\right)^2\right]. \end{aligned}\]

    Here \(M\) is the mass of the container and \(x\) its position, the two pendulum masses \(m\) hang on rods of length \(\ell\), and the \(d\) terms are the kinetic energy of extra mass that moves horizontally with each pendulum point.

    Starting at rest from \(\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 0.2\) rad, with \(m = 0.1\) kg, \(M = 1\) kg, \(d = 0.05\) kg and \(\ell = 5\) cm, fourth-order Runge–Kutta with a 1 ms step keeps the energy constant to \(6\times10^{-12}\) and agrees with a high-accuracy adaptive solver to \(6\times10^{-9}\) rad over 5 s. Nothing diverges, even with a step of 50 ms.

    Continuous model

    The surface is treated as a string of length \(l\) with both ends fixed, driven by a uniform oscillating force, starting flat and at rest. I replaced the poster’s damping term with ordinary linear damping:

    \[\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} + \gamma\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} - c^2\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} = \frac{F}{m}\cos(\omega t), \qquad u(0,t) = u(l,t) = 0.\]

    Expanding in the eigenfunctions \(\sin(n\pi x/l)\), the Sturm–Liouville approach mentioned on the poster, turns the equation into independent driven oscillators, and only the odd modes are forced. Without damping the solution is

    \[u(x,t)=\sum_{n\ \mathrm{odd}} \frac{4F}{n\pi m}\;\frac{\cos\omega t-\cos\omega_n t}{\omega_n^2-\omega^2}\;\sin\frac{n\pi x}{l},\qquad \omega_n=\frac{n\pi c}{l},\]

    and with damping each mode is a damped driven oscillator. The series agrees with a direct finite-difference simulation of the equation to \(4\times10^{-4}\) of the amplitude.

    This was joint work with Patrick Truong, Gregory Barbanel and Abdullah Fouzuddeen, whom I thank for their efforts and contributions. We are grateful to the Edinburgh Scientific Researchers Association for their support.

    Changes on 25 October 2026

    What was wrong. The introduction said the Navier–Stokes equations have no analytic solution. They do in special cases; what they lack is a general closed-form solution.

    What changed. The summary is rewritten to explain the two models more clearly. Previously titled “Group Research in fluid behaviours”.

    Poster corrections (26 October 2026)

    What was wrong.

    • The printed solution of the wave equation was wrong. It satisfies neither the equation nor its initial and boundary conditions, and its prefactor has the wrong units. Only one boundary condition was given, although the solution depends on the length \(l\).
    • The damping term \(-d\,(\partial u/\partial t)^2\) always has the same sign, so it does not oppose the motion.
    • Three terms of the Lagrangian were mistyped: \(m\ell\,\theta_1\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\) for \(m\ell^2\,\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\), \(m\ell\,\dot\theta_2^2\) for \(m\ell^2\,\dot\theta_2^2\), and \(\tfrac12 M\dot x\) for \(\tfrac12 M\dot x^2\). The equation of motion for \(x\) printed on the poster matches the corrected Lagrangian, so these were typing errors.
    • The poster said the two-pendulum simulation diverges because of Runge–Kutta errors. Integrating the equations again, from either the corrected or the printed Lagrangian, shows no divergence, so the problem was most likely in the original code.
    • Several typos, including “Disecrete model”.

    What changed. The poster itself is unchanged. The section Redoing the simulations above gives the corrected equations and new simulations of both models. The parameter values there are illustrative, since the poster did not give any.
