    Summer research 2024: the three-body problem · Part 4 of 11

    A weekly research log from my summer project at the University of Edinburgh, supervised by Prof. Jenni Smillie.

    ← Ellipses and the Two-Body Problem  ·  Understanding and Visualising the Shape Sphere →

    This post introduces the problem of two fixed centres, also known as Euler’s three-body problem: a small body moving under the gravity of two masses held fixed in place. Unlike the general three-body problem, it is integrable.

    Momentum conservation fixed

    In part 2 I noted that the total momentum in my simulation was drifting. The cause turned out to be a bug: an accumulator for the momentum components was not reset to zero at each time step. With the fix, the remaining error is at the level of floating-point round-off, about \(10^{-15}\):

    Set-up

    The two fixed masses \(m_\pm\) sit on the \(z\)-axis at \(z = \pm b\), and the third body moves in the \(x\)–\(z\) plane.

    Simulation

    To simulate the problem, I held two of the masses fixed and let the third move freely:

    The Lagrangian

    The natural coordinates are prolate spheroidal coordinates \((R, \sigma)\), which play the role of polar coordinates but with two foci:

    \[x = \pm \sqrt{R^2-b^2}\, \sin \sigma, \qquad z = R \cos \sigma .\]

    Writing \(\mathbf{r}(R, \sigma) = (x, z)\), the partial derivatives are

    \[\frac{\partial x}{\partial R} = \pm \frac{R \sin \sigma}{\sqrt{R^2 - b^2}}, \quad \frac{\partial z}{\partial R} = \cos \sigma, \qquad \frac{\partial x}{\partial \sigma} = \pm \sqrt{R^2 - b^2}\, \cos \sigma, \quad \frac{\partial z}{\partial \sigma} = -R \sin \sigma,\]

    so (taking the \(+\) sign)

    \[\mathbf{r}_R = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial R} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{R \sin \sigma}{\sqrt{R^2 - b^2}} \\ \cos \sigma \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{r}_\sigma = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \sigma} = \begin{pmatrix} \sqrt{R^2 - b^2}\, \cos \sigma \\ -R \sin \sigma \end{pmatrix} .\]

    The metric components \(g_{RR}= \mathbf{r}_R \cdot \mathbf{r}_R\), \(g_{R\sigma} = \mathbf{r}_R\cdot\mathbf{r}_\sigma\) and \(g_{\sigma\sigma} = \mathbf{r}_\sigma\cdot\mathbf{r}_\sigma\) come out as

    \[g_{RR}=\frac{R^2-b^2 \cos ^2 \sigma}{R^2-b^2}, \qquad g_{R \sigma}=0, \qquad g_{\sigma \sigma}=R^2-b^2 \cos ^2 \sigma .\]

    The distances to the two centres are \(R \mp b\cos\sigma\), so the potential energy per unit mass is

    \[V=-\mu\, \frac{R+\beta b \cos \sigma}{R^2-b^2 \cos ^2 \sigma}, \qquad \mu = G(m_+ + m_-), \qquad \beta=\frac{m_{+}-m_{-}}{m_{+}+m_{-}} .\]

    The Lagrangian per unit mass is therefore

    \[\mathcal{L} = T-V =\frac{1}{2}\, \frac{R^2-b^2 \cos ^2 \sigma}{R^2-b^2}\, \dot{R}^2+\frac{1}{2}\left(R^2-b^2 \cos ^2 \sigma\right) \dot{\sigma}^2+\mu\, \frac{R+\beta b \cos \sigma}{R^2-b^2 \cos ^2 \sigma} .\]

    Jacobi elliptic functions

    The equations of motion separate in these coordinates. Following the book, the angular motion reduces (after a change of independent variable) to an equation of the form

    \[S^{\prime 2}=\left(1-S^2\right)\left(1-k^2 S^2\right),\]

    whose solution is

    \[S=\operatorname{sn}(f+\omega \mid k).\]

    Here \(\operatorname{sn}\) is one of the Jacobi elliptic functions. Trigonometric functions are defined with reference to a circle, and the Jacobi elliptic functions generalise them to other conic sections, in particular the ellipse.

    To verify the solution, use

    \[\frac{d}{du} \operatorname{sn}(u \mid k) = \operatorname{cn}(u \mid k) \operatorname{dn}(u \mid k)\]

    together with the identities

    \[\operatorname{sn}^2(u \mid k) + \operatorname{cn}^2(u \mid k) = 1, \qquad \operatorname{dn}^2(u \mid k) + k^2 \operatorname{sn}^2(u \mid k) = 1 .\]

    With \(u = f + \omega\), these give \(\operatorname{cn}^2 u = 1 - S^2\) and \(\operatorname{dn}^2 u = 1 - k^2 S^2\), so

    \[S'^2 = \operatorname{cn}^2(u \mid k)\, \operatorname{dn}^2(u \mid k) = (1 - S^2)(1 - k^2 S^2) .\]

    Progress

    At the end of the first week, the simulation code is in good shape, ahead of my plan, and I have started thinking about the structure of the final report.
