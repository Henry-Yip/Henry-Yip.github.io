An Analysis of the Restricted Euler Problem Using Symplectic Integrators
Henry Yip and Jennifer M. Smillie
Edinburgh Student Journal of Science 1(3), 2025
doi:10.2218/esjs.10064 Journal page
Written up from my first research project, an eight-week summer project in 2024: an early, exploratory piece of work.

The Three-Body Problem is far from fully solved despite centuries of effort. The restricted Euler Problem is a special case in which two bodies are fixed in place, resulting in two Poisson-commuting conserved quantities, allowing the system to be fully integrable by the Liouville–Arnold theorem. We analysed the restricted Euler problem using an order-4 symplectic integrator, which conserves the Hamiltonian. We used this integrator to simulate the restricted Euler problem and recovered known orbits from the literature.

Computational statistical physics (2026): the 3D Heisenberg model

My final project for Computational Statistical Physics at ETH Zürich, which received full marks: a Monte Carlo study of the three-dimensional classical Heisenberg model, measuring the critical temperature and five critical exponents with Metropolis and Wolff cluster simulations on lattices up to 256³ spins, all within 1σ of the 3D O(3) universality class.

Summer research project (2025): synchronisation in 2D turbulence

I studied the synchronisation of small scales in two-dimensional turbulence driven by Kolmogorov forcing, using JAX-CFD, and checked my code against results in the literature. I am now extending the theory to include Coriolis effects on the beta-plane. The work is supervised by Dr Jacob Page, with assistance from Dr Dmitriy Zhigunov.

Summer research project (2024): the three-body problem

An eight-week project in the School of Physics and Astronomy, University of Edinburgh, supervised by Prof. Jenni Smillie.

The project had two aims: to reproduce and visualise important orbits of the three-body problem, and to understand two landmark theoretical results, Chenciner and Montgomery’s proof that the figure-eight orbit exists and the convergence of Sundman’s series. I wrote symplectic integrators up to fourth order, used them to study the Lagrange, figure-eight and Broucke–Hadjidemetriou–Hénon orbits and Euler’s problem of two fixed centres, and ended with a search for new well-behaved orbits.

Research problem: liquid slosh with pendulums and waves

This began as a group research project at the Edinburgh Scientific Researchers Association (ESRA), with Patrick Truong, Gregory Barbanel and Abdullah Fouzuddeen. Starting from a published single-pendulum model of liquid slosh in packaging machines, we looked at two ways of capturing the shape of the liquid surface rather than just its tilt: several coupled pendulums, and a wave equation for the surface.
